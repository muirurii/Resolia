Conciliation

Guidance toward common ground.

A neutral process in which the conciliator can take a more active role in helping parties identify possible solutions and areas of compromise.

Listen. Clarify. Find a way forward.

How conciliation works

A practical process for narrowing the distance.

  1. Understand the matter

    The conciliator learns about the dispute, the people involved and the outcome each party seeks.

  2. Clarify the issues

    The parties identify the areas of agreement, disagreement and practical concern.

  3. Explore options

    The conciliator may suggest possible approaches and help test realistic alternatives.

  4. Build compromise

    The process focuses on workable concessions and areas of common ground.

  5. Document the outcome

    Any agreement reached can be set out clearly for the parties.

Conciliation may be useful when:

  • Parties need active neutral guidance
  • Communication has stalled
  • Several practical options are available
  • A relationship may need to continue
  • Early resolution can prevent escalation
  • A flexible process is preferred
